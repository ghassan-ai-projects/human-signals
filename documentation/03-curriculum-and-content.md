# Curriculum and content inventory

## Content boundary

R1 is intentionally a small, complete curriculum. The catalog below is normative for coverage. Physiological statements in the supplied brief are starting hypotheses for authoring, not reviewed source material. Every real claim must complete the scientific workflow before public release. Synthetic fixtures must use fictional labels and stay out of public builds.

All released entities have stable IDs, Intro/Standard/Mechanism explanations, glossary coverage, context and relevant references. Public pages cannot display an advanced-depth placeholder. “Not covered here” is acceptable for genuinely out-of-scope biology, never as a substitute for required R1 content.

## Journeys: 9 complete lessons

Target presentation durations below are editorial budgets, not biological durations. Final duration can vary by ±20% if readability is preserved. All steps are manually inspectable.

|---|---|---|---|---:|

| j-sympathetic | A fast stress response | Distinguish neural communication and adrenal-medullary output; show separate sympathetic branches and timing caveats | 75 s | 2 |
| j-thyroid | Thyroid-axis regulation | Follow TRH/TSH and T3/T4 context; identify negative feedback; avoid equating both outputs | 90 s | 2 |
| j-fed | After a meal | Trace an authored nutrient/glucose context, insulin-related source/target relationships and regulation | 75 s | 2 |

| j-prolactin | Dopamine and endocrine regulation | Identify hypothalamic/pituitary context and inhibitory relationship; show why inhibition is a signed edge | 60 s | 2 |
| j-sleep | Light, timing and sleep | Follow selected circadian/light-related signaling and melatonin context; explicitly explain omitted contributors to sleep | 90 s | 2 |
| j-dopamine-motor | Dopamine in movement | Identify a motor pathway separately from other dopamine contexts; explain one bounded mechanism without implying global dopamine effects | 75 s | 2 |
| j-dopamine-learning | Dopamine in learning and motivation | Explain an authored pathway/context; distinguish evidence-backed mechanism from broad behavior association | 90 s | 2 |

Every journey must include an overview, prerequisites, trigger, source, communication, target, effect, regulation/feedback or a reviewed explanation of why a closed feedback loop is not depicted, context limits, causal transcript, evidence, completion summary and at least two optional checkpoint predictions. At least one prediction concerns mechanism or regulation rather than molecule-name recall.

The supplied six-stage teaching pattern is not a claim that every biological pathway is a closed loop. Do not fabricate feedback for motor or learning lessons simply to fill a template.

## Human States: 3 integrated lessons

| ID | Scenario | Required tracks | Distinct learning task |
|---|---|---|---|
| state-stress | A single illustrative acute stress episode, recovery included | Sympathetic neural/adrenal response; HPA response; selected target effects and regulation | Explain parallel pathways and relative timescales without treating all effects as cortisol-mediated |
| state-sleep | An illustrative transition into a dark/rest period | Environmental timing input; central timing pathway; melatonin-related communication; explicit note about other sleep regulation | Distinguish a time-of-day signal from sleep as a complex state |
| state-meal-fasting | An illustrative transition from a meal toward an interval without food | Nutrient context; insulin-related communication; glucagon-related communication; selected liver/tissue effects | Explain changing regulation without treating hormones as universal opposites |

Each state has 2–4 named tracks, a 120–180 second presentation, at least two optional predictions, all three depths and the same evidence/Why access as journeys. Tracks may reuse canonical relationships but have authored timing. State playback is not multiple independent journey players started together. The meal/fasting state must not silently extend to days of starvation; prolonged fasting is R2 content with separate review.

The generic comparison UI must also support every other pair of released signals using the same authored fields. A pair-specific essay is required only for the three curated pairs. No generated physiology is permitted to fill gaps.

## Anatomy coverage

The initial model needs a body shell plus selectable semantic regions for brain, hypothalamus, pituitary (with anterior distinction when used), pineal region, thyroid, left/right adrenals (cortex/medulla distinctions), pancreas (alpha/beta cell source distinctions in diagrams), heart, lungs, liver, skeletal muscle and adipose tissue. Immune targets may appear as a labeled distributed-system overlay if reviewed effects use them; never invent a single “immune organ.”

Brain detail adds brainstem, a motor-source/striatal context and a learning-source/target context such as those chosen by scientific review. Exact nuclei and route descriptions are authored and reviewed; the rendering interface accepts stable semantic IDs so assets do not dictate unsupported anatomy. A broad contextual region may be shown before detailed structures are approved, but the R1 motor and learning lessons still require distinct reviewed source/target anchors at release.

Peripheral tissues represented schematically must carry that label. Pancreatic cell detail is a 2D inset, not an anatomically literal organ-shaped region. No reproductive anatomy is required in R1; reserve the data model's ability to represent anatomical variants rather than building a male body as a universal template.

## Content authoring package

For each lesson, deliver:

1. Objective and prerequisite concept IDs; target depth and scenario assumptions.
2. Relationship inventory with one or more claim IDs for every edge, mechanism and comparison value.
3. Intro, Standard and Mechanism text. Suggested lengths: 40–90, 90–180 and 150–300 words for the overview; step captions 15–45 words. Readability matters more than hitting a count.
4. Storyboard: steps, tracks, presentation milliseconds, timing labels, highlights, captions, edge transitions and feedback.
5. Why roots for each edge and a finite explanation graph. Each edge needs at least one mechanism explanation and at least one deeper concept where pedagogically meaningful.
6. Evidence package with checked references, scope, limitations and review fingerprint.
7. Prediction prompts with option-specific feedback, reveal boundaries, objective and difficulty.
8. Text transcript, accessible diagram order, glossary definitions, asset/anchor requirements and reviewer checklist.

Each required lesson must contain at least one explicit limitation or simplification statement. Required source quality is defined by claim support, not a citation-count quota. More references cannot compensate for a missing source for a consequential claim.

## Curriculum order and coverage checks

Suggested route: communication basics → HPA → sympathetic → Stress → thyroid → fed → fasting → meal/fasting → dopamine endocrine → motor → learning → sleep. Users can jump freely; prerequisites are links, not access locks.

At least 24 checkpoint predictions are required: two per journey and two per state. In addition, author six transfer prompts: two on feedback, two on parallel routes/timing, two on context-dependent targets. Assign held-out forms as described in document 09. Every signal must participate in a released relationship, every journey must have an entry from at least one signal or state, and every state must have an entry from Home or the state catalog.

## Expansion policy

R2 content has a separate readiness checklist. Exercise, sexual response, prolonged fasting and serotonin must not appear as implemented state cards until fully authored and reviewed. Sexual-response content, if later released, is educational physiology with anatomical and response variability represented; its addition does not authorize collection of users' sexual or health information. The coding agent should not prebuild those user flows during R1.
